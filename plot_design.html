Does Simple Gamification Impact the Cognitive Burden of Filling in Surveys?

We are using two datasets: a pilot study and a survey gamification experiment.

The pilot dataset includes physiological signals from 11 volunteers captured by three wearable devices: Empatica E4, Samsung Galaxy Watch4, and Muse S EEG headband, in two conditions:

  • Stroop Test: A cognitive task where participants name the ink color of a word instead of reading the word itself. This requires focus and is mentally demanding. You’ve already experienced this earlier in the project!
  • Baseline Condition: Participants sat in a relaxed position for three minutes with no distractions or tasks.
This dataset helps evaluate whether consumer-grade smartwatches can detect cognitive load during demanding tasks.

The survey gamification dataset includes physiological signals from 13 volunteers using the same three wearable devices across two sessions. In both sessions, participants completed a cognitive task, took a break, and then filled out four work and well-being surveys (GRIT, PANAS, HAPPINESS, and NFR). The surveys were either gamified or non-gamified, with participants split into two groups:
  • Group 1: Gamified surveys first, non-gamified in the second session.
  • Group 2: Non-gamified surveys first, then gamified.
Sessions were spaced two weeks apart.

This dataset explores whether simple gamification reduces the cognitive burden of survey completion.

Empatica E4 Wristband

The Empatica E4 is a wrist-worn, research-grade wearable and a Class IIa Medical Device designed for physiological monitoring. It measures key biometric signals, including:

  • Blood Volume Pulse (BVP): Used to derive Heart Rate Variability (HRV) and Inter-Beat Interval (IBI), providing insights into cardiovascular activity.
  • Electrodermal Activity (EDA): Captures skin conductance, reflecting emotional arousal and stress responses.
  • Skin Temperature (TEMP): Monitors changes in body temperature, which can indicate stress, activity levels, or environmental adaptation.

This device is widely used in research on cognitive load, stress, and emotional regulation. Understanding these signals will help in exploring their variations in different conditions.

Samsung Galaxy Watch4

The Samsung Galaxy Watch4 is a consumer-grade smartwatch running on Wear OS. It was used in this study to collect Photoplethysmogram (PPG) signals, which measure blood flow variations and can be analyzed to assess cognitive load.

  • Blood Volume Pulse (BVP): A PPG-derived signal that tracks changes in blood volume and heart activity. The Galaxy Watch4 captures PPG Green signals using the Samsung Privileged Health SDK.
The collected signal is stored and available for further analysis below.
Note: Unlike the Empatica E4, the Samsung Galaxy Watch4 only records Blood Volume Pulse (BVP).

Conclusion

Through interactive visualizations, we explored how different physiological signals respond to cognitive load conditions across multiple wearable devices.

Our analysis provides insights into:

  • Variability in physiological responses: Different biometric signals (e.g., BVP, EDA, Temperature) react uniquely under cognitive stress.
  • Device comparisons: Observing trends across multiple wearables allows us to assess their effectiveness in monitoring cognitive load.
  • Individual-level insights: By enabling participant-specific visualizations, we capture personalized physiological trends over time.

By integrating real-time interactivity and smooth transitions, we provide a dynamic tool for exploring the nuanced relationship between cognitive load and biometric data.
